# Judging Rubric - IEEE YP Industry Hackathon

**Event:** Autonomous Intelligence for Industrial Innovation
**Dates:** October 2–4, 2026 | Collision Space, Hunter Hub, University of Calgary
**Hosted by:** IEEE Southern Alberta Section Young Professionals

Judges score projects using weighted criteria totaling **100%**. Option A (bring your own problem) and Option B (prepared case) use **this same rubric**, so every team is assessed on a level playing field.

> **How judging works:** each team gets a **5-minute pitch + 3-minute Q&A**. Score what you **see and hear** in the presentation: the problem, why it matters, how the software solves it, the live demo with real output, and a clear architecture diagram.

## Criteria & Evaluation Guidance

### 1. Autonomous Reasoning + Data-Driven Decisions - 30%

Does the software think for itself - using data to make decisions and improve?

This is the "go beyond a static app" criterion. Show **any clear form** of autonomous or automated decision-making: agentic automation, an ML prediction loop, a simulation, an optimizer, auto-ranking / auto-flagging / auto-scheduling, or a rule the software adjusts on its own. Pick the form that fits your problem.

**Judges would look for (in the live demo):**

- Data goes in, a decision or action comes out (e.g. a ranking, flag, schedule, dispatch list, route, or score).
- The team shows **one improvement round**: first result vs. improved result after the software revised something (a cutoff, threshold, ranking, weight, route, or plan), compared against a simple baseline (the "lazy" or obvious answer).
- A simple diagram or one-sentence explanation of the loop: what data informed the decision, and what changed.

*What great looks like: the judge can say "the software decided X from data Y, then improved it to Z."*

### 2. Real Industrial Problem & Relevance - 20%

Does the team solve a real industrial problem inside its stream - one worth solving?

**Judges would look for:**

- A clear problem statement that fits the team's stream, with an ideal end user - a real-world industrial user (e.g. a City business unit, plant operator, dispatcher, nurse, or crew lead).
- Why this problem matters now: what breaks, costs money, wastes time, or creates risk if it is not solved. Real or public data grounds the problem.
- **Problem fit:** teams are strongly encouraged to **talk to a real industry person, operator, or mentor** during the weekend and report one line of what they learned (who they spoke to, what pain was confirmed). This is a bonus, not a penalty if unavailable.

*What great looks like: the problem, the industrial user affected, and why it matters are all immediately clear.*

### 3. Execution & Software Architecture - 20%

Does the software actually work, and can the team explain how it is built?

Judges score whether the solution runs and produces a **real, visible output** from the software - not slides or mockups alone.

**Judges would look for:**

- A **live working demo** where an input produces a real output on screen (ranking, flag, schedule, prediction, assignment, or recommendation). A short backup video or screenshots are fine if the live demo fails.
- A clear architecture explanation with a **diagram, data-flow map, or system sketch**: the main building blocks, how data moves from input to decision to output, and how the whole app fits together.
- The reasoning behind the approach: why the team chose this design and toolset, and why it is practical in terms of **efficiency, cost, and ease of use**.
- The solution goes **beyond a static app**: it automates, decides, predicts, ranks, or simulates something - it does not just display information.

*What great looks like: the software runs, the output is clear, and one diagram shows how the whole system works and why that design was chosen.*

### 4. Commercialization in Industry - 15%

Could this actually be deployed and scaled in industry?

**Judges would look for:**

- A clear **value proposition and solution fit**: who benefits, who saves time/money or reduces risk, and why this solution is better than the current way of working. Teams are encouraged to validate this with a **quick conversation with an industry person or mentor**.
- A realistic **deployment approach**: where it would run first (a pilot site or team), what it connects to, and what happens next.
- A convincing **scalability plan**: how the solution grows from this weekend prototype to wider use - more users, sites, or data, at a cost and effort that stays practical. The team should show the solution can scale up, not just work once.

*What great looks like: the judge believes a real team could pilot this next and scale it after that.*

### 5. Presentation & Demo Quality - 15%

Can the team explain their work clearly in the time given?

**Judges would look for (5-minute pitch + 3-minute Q&A):**

- A clear flow: the problem → why it matters → approach and architecture → live demo with output → result vs. baseline and value. You could follow the template that was provided during the pitch workshop.
- The demo is the centerpiece: show the software working live.
- The team finishes on time and answers questions directly.

*What great looks like: an 8-minute slot any judge can follow.*

---

## Where to focus

To keep judging fast and fair across many teams, keep the focus on the problem, the data-informed decision, the working output, and the value. A crisp pitch that covers these four things scores best.

- Pointers to datasets used (do **not** upload multi-GB raw data; document download steps)
